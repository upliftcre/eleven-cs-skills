# The Market Authority Sentence

One sentence naming the place they own. It does two jobs at once: it organizes their decisions internally, and it is the exact string humans repeat and machines parse externally.

## Get the honest version first

Before anything else, ask what they actually chase today — hedges included. Write it down verbatim. *"Whatever comes in around the metro, mostly office, some land"* is a real answer and a useful one.

Read it back to them. That is precisely how it sounds to a stranger, and to every machine evaluating them.

## Then build the real one

Place-led. Authority claimed. No hedges.

> *"I am the authority on commercial real estate in the I-35 corridor."*

Test it four ways. All four must pass.

1. **Could a stranger find that geography on a map?** "The Southeast" technically resolves and means nothing. Nobody believes regional dominance and no machine can verify it.
2. **Does the asset focus follow the place rather than lead it?** *"Industrial in Southern California, mostly"* fails twice — it hedges, and it leads with the asset.
3. **Would they say it to the dominant incumbent in that market?**
4. **Can they say it out loud without hedging?** *Mostly, primarily, and surrounding areas* are unmade decisions announcing themselves.

If the sentence fails, do not scold. Have them write the honest version of what they actually do, then ask the real question: is that scattered practice a choice, or did it happen to them? Both answers are allowed. Only one is a strategy.

## The argument they will raise

*"So I should abandon my specialty and become a generalist?"*

No — and that objection is old-playbook programming. The choice was never specialist-or-generalist. Those were the only two positions the old rule could see, because it assumed knowledge was the differentiator and knowledge came in asset-shaped packages.

The actual position is **Market Authority**: commands a geography, works every asset class inside it, and sees the cross-asset trades a lane-picker's filter never admits.

They keep all their asset knowledge. They re-point it. *"The industrial guy who happens to work in this market"* becomes *"the authority on this market who does a lot of industrial."* Same knowledge, opposite position.

## Three things worth telling them

**Asset information commoditized; asset judgment did not.** Do not say "asset expertise is dead" — a good broker will know that is wrong in three seconds. What changed is the toll they used to pay to acquire the information.

**The lane was always a platform strategy.** Asset specialization pays when the client universe is national and institutional, and reaching it needs capital relationships, a research department, and procurement clearance an independent cannot assemble. They were sold an institutional play without the institution. That is why the specialist-or-generalist argument has felt unwinnable their whole career — one of the positions was never on the table.

**A record built on a place concentrates; a record built on a lane dilutes.** A machine characterizes on the dominant signal. Every deal inside one named geography points at the same coordinate. Chase a lane across four metros and the record gets *less* legible the more successful they become — and nothing about winning a deal feels like a cost.

## Then audit the pipeline

How much of their active book sits inside the sentence they just claimed? How much outside?

No judgment — measurement. The gap is next quarter's prospecting map.

## For two specific readers

**Coming from residential:** they arrive with the two things everyone else is trying to build — real street-level geographic knowledge, and no asset-lane baggage to un-learn. But their record points at houses, and a mixed record is worse than a thin one. A machine will read them as a residential agent doing some commercial, and offer to find someone else.

Tell them plainly: **do not bank the old transactions.** Bank the market instead — corridor analysis, submarket commentary, what the interchange does to three parcels, which sites the sewer extension unlocked. Commercial judgment demonstrated without a commercial transaction claimed, and available this week.

**New to the industry:** they are choosing on a blank page, which nobody else gets. Pick narrow — narrow enough that a decade of attention would genuinely exhaust it — and accumulate there from week one. Take whatever work comes; they should. But accumulate in one place.

## The workbook rule

If the advisor uses the companion workbook, **never write to the .xlsx file.**

Its formulas feed a dashboard across thirteen tabs, and a library will write straight through Excel's sheet protection without warning. A clobbered formula produces no error — it produces a wrong number that looks right.

Output a paste-ready block instead. Tell them which tab and which column, and let them paste it. That takes them four seconds and it cannot break anything.

If they explicitly ask you to write the file anyway, say what the risk is first, and tell them to run `verify_repair.py --repair` afterwards.
