# The Backward Trace

Start at the number they intend to finish the year with. Divide backward through their own rates. Arrive at how many conversations have to exist this month.

It takes one page and it is the difference between a plan and a resolution.

## Get four numbers

1. **GCI goal, next twelve months.**
2. **Average net GCI per closing** — net of splits, from their last ten deals. Not gross. Not a good year.
3. **Contract-to-closing rate** — of executed contracts, what fraction actually funded?
4. **Client-to-contract and conversation-to-client rates** — over a trailing six months.

**If they cannot state their conversion rates, stop.** That is the finding, and it is pass/fail with no partial credit. A feel is not a rate. Tell them so, then run the trace on estimates clearly flagged as guesses, and tell them next quarter it will not be permitted.

If their records do not reach back six months, run it on one quarter and flag the gaps. Even one quarter of real rates beats zero.

## The six steps

1. Start with the GCI goal.
2. Divide by average commission per closing → **required closings**
3. Divide by contract-to-closing rate → **required contracts**
4. Divide by client-to-contract rate → **required signed clients**
5. Divide by conversation-to-client rate → **required qualified conversations**
6. Compare against what they actually have. **The gap is the business plan.**

Worked, illustrative only:

> $500K goal ÷ $25K average = 20 closings. ÷ 50% = 40 contracts. ÷ 80% = 50 clients. ÷ 40% = **125 qualified conversations across the year**, about ten entering qualification a month. If today's pipeline holds thirty, the gap is visible in one page of arithmetic.

## Three levers, and only three

When the gap appears — and it will, on the first run — there are exactly three responses:

**Raise activity.** More qualified conversations entering the pipeline. But check first whether the existing hours are leaking; adding hours to a leaky system just doubles the leak.

**Improve conversion.** Usually the highest-payoff lever, and the trace tells them which stage pays best. Run it both ways: what does one more point of conversation-to-client save in required volume, versus one more point of contract-to-closing? Earlier stages usually win, because improvements there cascade through every division downstream.

**Adjust the goal.** Not surrender. A goal that ignores measured rates is not ambitious, it is arbitrary. A reduced goal is fine if it comes with a dated re-escalation. What is fatal is the silent version — quietly lowering expectations without telling anyone, including themselves.

**What is not on the list:** working harder while measuring nothing. Say that out loud if they reach for it.

## Warn them before you show them

The first run rarely feels good. It usually feels like being audited by yourself.

They will find rates lower than they assumed, stages they cannot count at all, and a goal that needs a pipeline several times the size of the one they built. Advisors who run this honestly tend to go quiet for a day.

That discomfort is the instrument working. And there is a payoff inside it: an advisor who knows his numbers negotiates his own career differently. He can tell a team lead exactly what his pipeline supports. He can size a hire against required conversation volume instead of a feeling. He can survive a slow month without panicking, because the trace shows whether it was structural or statistical.

## Two honesty rules for the inputs

**Count at the moment of transition, not the moment of hope.** A client is signed when paper exists, not when they say they are ready.

**Never round in their favor.** An advisor who counts "basically closed" deals as closings is running a trace on fiction and will plan a year on it.

## The quarterly review

First Monday after the quarter closes. One hour.

- **Ten minutes** — score every instrument fresh, before looking at last quarter's numbers.
- **Fifteen** — set them side by side. Looking for direction, not perfection.
- **Twenty** — trace the weakest link through the loop. What does it do to its neighbors two quarters out?
- **Ten** — pick the weakest C and write one sentence: *"This quarter I work ___, using ___, measured by ___."*

**When the arithmetic and the self-assessment disagree, believe the arithmetic.**

## Where the gap points

- Not enough conversations entering → Contacts. Velocity and cadence.
- Conversations that do not sign → Credibility and Clients. Thin proof, or an exclusivity ask never made.
- Signed but not papered, or dying on deadlines → Contracts.
- Closings that feed nothing → Closings, and it is why their cost per conversation keeps rising instead of falling.

One number, one diagnosis, one lever.
