# The Competency Audit

A license certifies that someone is legally permitted to practice. It says nothing about whether they still can.

This is not a self-rating. Ask someone to score themselves 0–5 on negotiation and they will pick a 4, and so will the advisor who loses every trade at the table. Neither number means anything.

**Ask for evidence instead.**

## The question, seven times

For each capability, one question:

> **Can you point to a specific deal or engagement in the last ninety days where you performed this, and name the outcome that verified it?**

Not "I'm good at this." A deal, a date, and what happened.

Three possible answers, and only three:

- **Evidenced** — they named the deal and the verifying outcome.
- **Stale** — they can name one, but it is older than a year. Craft has a half-life; a 2024 example is a claim about 2024.
- **Absent** — they cannot produce one. Not an insult. The finding.

## The seven

| Capability | What an evidenced answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| **Market mastery** | Caught a zoning, entitlement or infrastructure issue before the professionals did |
| **Financial fluency** | Built an analysis that survived a sophisticated counterparty |
| **Deal anatomy** | Predicted where a live deal would break, and was right |
| **Contract literacy** | Spotted a clause-level issue before counsel raised it |
| **Building fundamentals** | Read a physical condition that changed a number |
| **Market analytics** | Interpreted data in a way later borne out |
| **Negotiation** | Held a position under pressure and can name what it saved |

Push when an answer is soft. *"I know my market well"* is a fail. *"The Rayford pursuit in February — I flagged the drainage easement before the buyer's engineer, and it changed the price"* is a pass.

## Name one constraint, not a list

Whichever is Stale or Absent becomes the quarter's constraint. If there are two, take the one their recent deals actually exposed — not the one that embarrasses them most.

One concrete development action per week, calendared before deal work. Reading one lease clause deeply. Rebuilding last quarter's best model with current rate assumptions. Walking a building with an engineer and asking questions until the visit costs them something in humility.

## The Craft Investment Floor

Two numbers, in writing:

- **Hours per week** of deliberate study. Not deal work that happens to teach.
- **Dollars per year** on craft — coursework, designations, conferences chosen for curriculum rather than location.

Numbers they would defend out loud to someone who has spent a lifetime selling. Below-floor weeks get logged, not rationalized.

## The thing worth saying out loud

An airline captain's license never expires — his authority to fly does. Every few months he is checked against defined standards by someone whose job is to fail him. Thirty years of experience buys no exemption. A surgeon re-credentials. An accountant re-tests.

Their license renews when they pay a fee and sit through hours nobody grades.

**Nobody is coming to check whether they are still good at this.** Not the brokerage — it gets paid on closings, not competence. Not the client — that asymmetry is what they were hired to close. Not the state.

That is the problem with Competency, and it is also the whole opportunity: it is true of every advisor they compete against. In a field where nobody audits anybody, the advisor who audits himself is playing a different game.

## The order matters

Competency sits under everything else. Banking proof of expertise requires expertise to bank — a structured record amplifying shallow craft does not build authority, it accelerates exposure. Conversations convert on answers. Contracts close because someone in the room actually understands them.

If they are strong everywhere else and Absent here, say so directly. The rest of the system will make a mediocre advisor findable faster, which is not a favor.

## The tracker rule

The advisor's workbook is **The Eleven C's Tracker**: `10-eleven-cs-tracker.xlsx`, from the book's System Folder download. It has fourteen tabs: Read Me, Dashboard, C1 to C11, and The Feeder.

**Never write to the .xlsx file. There is no exception.** Not when they ask, not when they insist, not when they say they're busy. Calculated cells feed the Dashboard. A write can overwrite one without any warning, and a broken formula shows no error. It shows a wrong number that looks right.

Give them a paste-ready block instead, every time:

- One line per value, in this shape: `Tab › Cell · Label: value`.
- Use only the cells in the map below. Every other cell is a label or a calculation, so never put a value in one.
- In a log, use the next empty row. Leave no blank rows between entries.
- Where the map shows words in quotes, use one of them exactly. That cell has a dropdown.
- Percentages go in as plain numbers: 2 means 2%.
- Dates go in as dates: 2026-10-01.

If they push back, say this once: *"Pasting takes you a few seconds. If I write the file, I can break a formula and neither of us would see it."* Then give the block again.

If they don't use the tracker, skip the block. The instrument still runs.
